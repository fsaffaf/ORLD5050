Intuitive and Analytical Decision Making

Overview

Decision making involves two closely connected types of thinking: intuitive thinking and analytical thinking.

Intuitive Thinking

A fast, automatic form of thinking based on patterns, experience, and immediate impressions.

Analytical Thinking

A slower, more deliberate form of thinking that involves carefully evaluating information, evidence, and possible outcomes.

In many Western countries, such as the United States, Australia, and England, decision making tends to favor analytical reasoning over intuitive thinking.

In contrast, Eastern countries such as China, India, and Japan have largely preserved the role of intuition alongside analytical thought.

Research suggests these processes are not separate but work together during decision making. Intuition helps narrow options quickly, while analytical thinking helps evaluate them more thoroughly.

Heuristics

The human brain cannot process all available information at once, so it uses mental shortcuts called heuristics.

Heuristics

Heuristics are mental shortcuts that allow faster decision making by focusing on the most relevant information while ignoring less critical details.

The Benefits

These shortcuts help us make efficient decisions in complex environments where time and information are limited.

The Limitations

Heuristics may ignore important details and lead to errors when situations are unfamiliar or complex.

The Solution

Heuristics should be evaluated to determine when they are useful and when they introduce risk.

Heuristic What It Means Helpful Example Harmful Example
Well-Known Heuristic Choosing the option you recognize. Picking a trusted laptop brand. Choosing medicine due to ads.
Popularity Heuristic Choosing what many people choose. Using a popular study app. Following low-quality trends.
Similarity Heuristic Using past similar experiences. Reusing a study method. Hiring someone based on similarity.
Auto-Pilot Heuristic Accepting default options. Using secure app defaults. Ignoring privacy settings.
Emotion Heuristic Decisions based on feelings. Avoiding risky situations. Impulsive decisions.
Recent Heuristic Overweighting recent events. Avoiding accident-prone roads. Overestimating rare risks.

Biases

Biases are intuitive influences on decision making that often operate outside awareness.

Term Definition Helpful Harmful
Confirmation Bias Focusing on supporting evidence. Quick filtering. Ignoring contradictions.
Sunk Cost Bias Continuing due to past investment. Persistence when success likely. Continuing failing plans.
Overconfidence Bias Overestimating ability. Encourages action. Underestimating risk.

Key Takeaways

  • Decision making involves both intuition and analysis.
  • Heuristics are intentional shortcuts.
  • Biases are often unconscious.
  • Both can be helpful or harmful.
