Subclaims

Understanding Subclaims

A main claim is the overall position you want your audience to accept. Subclaims are the individual reasons that explain why someone should accept that main claim.

A strong argument often includes three subclaims. Each subclaim should provide a different reason for accepting the main claim rather than repeating the same idea in different words.

Apply This to Your Own Main Claim

When developing your argument, ask yourself:

Why should someone accept my main claim?

Identify three distinct answers. Each answer should become a subclaim that supports your overall position.

Before moving forward, check your work:

  • Does each subclaim support the main claim?
  • Does each subclaim provide a different reason?
  • Are the subclaims specific enough to investigate through research?
  • Do the three subclaims work together to support the overall position?

In Ethical, Evidence-Based Decision Making (EEBDM), this structure helps you organize a complex position into three clear, distinct reasons. Your subclaims establish the major points your overall argument needs to address.
