Ethics

Lecture 4

Overview

A key part of Ethical Evidence-Based Decision Making (EEBDM) is ethics. Sometimes there is no clearly “right” choice, and we must balance competing values to decide on a course of action that can be ethically defended.

Combining ethical thinking with evidence-based reasoning helps us solve real-world problems more effectively. It increases the chances that our decisions are both morally responsible and well-informed.

A common challenge is that people often place their morals and personal values above evidence. When beliefs clash with facts, individuals may ignore or reinterpret evidence to fit their views. Understanding this tension is essential for strong decision making.

People’s ideas of what is morally desirable can differ based on culture, society, and personal values. As a result, individuals may reach different ethical conclusions even when looking at the same evidence.

Key Concepts

Term Definition
Ethics The study of moral principles used to evaluate right and wrong.
Ethical Perspective A formal set of principles used to evaluate ethical decisions.
Morals Personal or cultural beliefs about what is right and wrong.
Personal Values What matters to us individually, including goals and preferences.
Hedonic Motivation The tendency to seek pleasure and avoid discomfort.
Ethical Satisficing Making decisions that balance ethics and evidence within real-world limits, while remaining aware of bias and impact on others.

The Six Ethical Perspectives

Ethical perspectives provide different ways to evaluate decisions. Considering multiple perspectives leads to more balanced and defensible choices.

Perspective Key Question Main Focus
People’s Rights Does this respect basic rights? Freedom, safety, privacy, consent
Fairness Is this fair to everyone? Justice, equality, what people deserve
Consequences Does this create the best outcome? Benefits vs. harms
Community Well-Being How does this affect the group? Common good, shared resources, trust
Character Does this reflect who I want to be? Integrity, honesty, moral consistency
Relationships Does this consider others’ needs? Empathy, care, maintaining relationships

Steps for Making an Ethical Decision

Ethical Decision Process

  1. Identify the ethical issue.
  2. Gather relevant and reliable evidence.
  3. Evaluate the decision using all six ethical perspectives.
  4. Select the most ethically defensible and evidence-supported choice.

Why This Matters

  • Helps you navigate complex decisions with no clear “right” answer.
  • Improves the ethical quality of your decisions.
  • Encourages balanced thinking across multiple perspectives.
  • Reduces bias and strengthens accountability.

Key Takeaways

  • Ethics and evidence must work together in decision making.
  • People may interpret the same facts differently based on values.
  • Using multiple ethical perspectives leads to stronger decisions.
  • Good decisions are both ethically defensible and evidence-based.
