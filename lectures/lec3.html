Argument Structure

Understanding how arguments are built is essential to Ethical, Evidence-Based Decision Making (EEBDM).

What is an Argument?

Argument: An organized set of reasons that support a conclusion.

In academic and professional settings, arguments help us justify decisions, evaluate information, and communicate clearly.

Basic Argument Structure

Basic Argument: A claim supported by one or more premises.

Component Definition Example
Claim The main point or conclusion being argued. Plastic bags should be banned.
Premise A reason that supports the claim. Plastic bags harm marine ecosystems.

Putting It Together

Example Argument:

Plastic bags should be banned (claim) because they harm marine ecosystems and contribute to long-term environmental damage (premises).

Why This Matters

  • Helps you think critically and logically.
  • Improves clarity in writing and presentations.
  • Supports stronger, evidence-based decisions.
  • Allows you to evaluate others’ reasoning.

Key Takeaways

  • Arguments structure reasoning and communication.
  • A basic argument includes a claim and supporting premises.
  • Stronger arguments rely on clear, relevant evidence.
