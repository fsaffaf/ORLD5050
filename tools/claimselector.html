Main Claim Builder

Choose your graduate program, identify an action you want to recommend, select three reasons supporting that action, and identify who should act. Use the dropdown suggestions or type your own answers at every step.

Step 1. Which graduate program are you in?

Your program determines which suggestions appear in the following steps.

Step 2. What should be done?

Choose a specific action, policy, or practice that an organization should adopt, change, or discontinue. You can use a suggestion or enter an action of your own.

I think that [WHO] should [DO WHAT], because [WHY 1], [WHY 2], and [WHY 3].
OR

Start with an action verb, such as require, establish, evaluate, implement, or revise. If you type an answer, it will be used instead of the dropdown selection.

Step 3. Why should this action be taken?

Choose three different reasons that explain why your recommendation may be beneficial. You may use the suggestions, write your own reasons, or combine both approaches.

The reasons for this recommendation are [WHY 1], [WHY 2], and [WHY 3].
OR
OR
OR

All three reasons must be completed and different from one another. Try to choose reasons that support your recommendation in distinct ways.

Step 4. Who should take action?

Identify the person, team, department, or organization with the responsibility or authority to carry out your recommendation. Choose a suggestion or identify your own decision-maker.

I think that [WHO] should [DO WHAT], because [WHY 1], [WHY 2], and [WHY 3].
OR

Identify a decision-maker who could realistically carry out the action you selected.

Your Complete Main Claim

Check your claim. Does the selected person or organization have the authority to take the recommended action? Is the action specific? Do your three reasons provide distinct justifications?

Check your evidence. Your reasons are proposed justifications, not proven facts. Look for credible research, professional standards, government publications, industry reports, or public case studies to evaluate and support each reason.

Keep the topic researchable. Choose an issue that can be investigated using publicly available information. Avoid topics that require confidential company records, private employee data, or access to an organization you do not have.

Remember: This claim is a starting point for your argument. Revise it as needed after reviewing the available evidence.
