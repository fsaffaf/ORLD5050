Article Research Notes Generator

Purpose: Use this tool to organize information from each article you decide to keep for your research paper.

Read the article, answer the questions, and record information in your own words. Use the dropdown menus to connect evidence to the specific subclaim it supports.

Important: Record direct quotations accurately. You will use these notes when writing your argumentative paper.

1. Article Information

2. Your Three Subclaims

Enter the three subclaims from your argument map. These will appear in the evidence dropdown menus below so you can connect each quotation to the correct part of your argument.

3. Purpose of the Article

Identify what the authors wanted to investigate, examine, or communicate.

4. Short Summary of the Article

Write a short summary of the article. Focus on its main findings or key points.

5. Evidence That Supports My Argument

Identify facts, statistics, or quotations from experts in the article that could support your argument. Your direct quotation should include at least one of these types of information. Connect each quotation to the specific subclaim it supports.

Remember: A quotation alone does not explain why your argument is correct. You must explain how the evidence supports the specific subclaim you select.

Add as many pieces of evidence as you need. You do not need to find a specific number of quotations in every article.

6. Limitations and Considerations

The questions below change based on the source type you selected. Consider what might affect the source's accuracy, credibility, relevance, or usefulness. You do not need to answer questions that do not apply.

Select a source type in Section 1 to see relevant questions.
